The Pomodoro Technique for Exam Prep

The Pomodoro Technique is simple: study in a fixed block, then take a short, real break. No half-focused four-hour stretch with your phone next to you — a clean block of attention, followed by a clean stop. The name comes from a tomato-shaped kitchen timer, but the mechanism is what matters: a deadline short enough that starting doesn't feel heavy, and a break short enough that you come back.

Why 30 minutes works for JEE/NEET prep

Most Pomodoro guides default to 25 minutes; PadhleBeta rooms run a 30-minute focus block instead, because a single JEE physics numerical or a NEET biology diagram-heavy topic often needs a few extra minutes to actually get into. Long enough to finish a real chunk of work, short enough that a 3-minute break still feels earned rather than a distraction.

Why doing it in a group changes things

A timer on your own phone is easy to pause, skip, or quietly ignore. A timer that a whole room is already running on is not — studying alongside other people adds a light social pressure that solo study apps can't replicate, without anyone actually watching you. That's the entire reason PadhleBeta's rooms exist instead of just shipping a standalone timer.

What happens on the break

When the 3-minute break starts, a quick poll asks the room whether they actually focused during the last block, and chat opens up. Both close again the moment the next focus block starts — so the break is a real pause, not a slow slide back into scrolling.
