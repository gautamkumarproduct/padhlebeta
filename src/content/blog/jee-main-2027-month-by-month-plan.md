JEE Main rewards consistency and problem-solving speed. This plan focuses on both, broken into the phases that actually work for most students.

## What JEE Main 2027 actually tests

Before planning, get clear on what you are preparing for. JEE Main is:

- **Numerical-answer dominant** (75% of questions). No options to help you out.
- **Concept application heavy.** Memorising formulas is necessary but not sufficient.
- **Time-pressured.** 90 questions in 180 minutes. You need ~2 minutes per question including marking.
- **NCERT-aligned for Chemistry.** For Physics and Maths, you go beyond NCERT significantly.

## Phase 1: Foundation (Months 1–5)

Five months is the right window for building foundations without burning out.

### Mathematics (heaviest weight in JEE)

- **Algebra first.** Sequences and series, complex numbers, quadratic equations, binomial theorem, permutations and combinations, probability.
- **Coordinate geometry next.** Straight lines, circles, conic sections. These are heavily tested.
- **Calculus last.** Limits, continuity, differentiation, integration. This is the largest chapter — give it 6 weeks minimum.
- **Vectors and 3D geometry** in parallel with calculus.

### Physics

- **Mechanics first** (Class 11). It is roughly 35% of JEE Main Physics. Newton's laws, work-energy, rotational mechanics, fluid mechanics, SHM, gravitation.
- **Modern physics** and **optics** in the next slot.
- **Electrostatics and current electricity** as the third block.
- **Thermodynamics and waves** to close out Class 11 topics.

### Chemistry

- **Physical chemistry first.** Mole concept, atomic structure, chemical bonding, thermodynamics, equilibrium, electrochemistry, kinetics.
- **Organic chemistry second.** GOC, isomerism, hydrocarbons, haloalkanes, alcohols/phenols/ethers, aldehydes/ketones, amines, biomolecules.
- **Inorganic chemistry last.** NCERT is the source of truth here. Read it twice.

### Daily rhythm (Phase 1)
- 7–9 hours.
- One main subject per day (4–5 hours) + touch-ups on the other two (1 hour each).
- One weekly test (topic-wise, not full syllabus).
- Sunday off, or Sunday revision only.

## Phase 2: Application and speed (Months 6–9)

Now you have the fundamentals. Time to **build speed**.

- **Daily problem sets:** 25–40 problems per day, time-bound.
- **Mock tests on alternate Sundays.** Full-length, simulated exam conditions.
- **Subject-specific deep dives.** Identify the 2–3 chapters per subject that are tripping you up and spend an extra week on each.

### Chapter weight cheat sheet (approximate)

**Maths high-weight chapters:** Coordinate geometry, calculus (definite integrals, differential equations), probability, vectors & 3D.

**Physics high-weight chapters:** Mechanics (modern physics & SHM), electrodynamics, optics, modern physics.

**Chemistry high-weight chapters:** Equilibrium, electrochemistry, organic chemistry (GOC, hydrocarbons, carbonyl compounds), coordination compounds.

Spend more time on these.

### Daily rhythm (Phase 2)
- 8–10 hours.
- 50% problem practice, 30% revision, 20% mock tests.

## Phase 3: Mock-heavy (Months 10–11)

You should be scoring in your target range. The goal is to **lock it in**.

- **Two full mocks per week.** Analyse every single one.
- **Error log.** Spreadsheet or notebook — every wrong answer, the chapter, the reason (concept / silly mistake / time), the fix.
- **Re-attempt previous mocks.** A surprising number of "silly mistakes" repeat. Identifying them is half the battle.

### Daily rhythm (Phase 3)
- 9–11 hours.
- 50% revision, 30% new problems on weak areas, 20% mocks.

## Phase 4: Final sprint (Final 4–6 weeks)

No new chapters. Revision only.

- **Formula sheets.** One per subject. Memorise.
- **NCERT Chemistry recap.** Read it cover to cover. Inorganic especially.
- **Mock + light revision.** 3 mocks a week, the rest of the time for revision.
- **Sleep.** 7–8 hours. The week before the exam is not for cramming.

## What separates 99 percentilers from 95 percentilers

Three things, in order:

1. **Speed without silly mistakes.** Most students who fall short of their target lose marks not to hard questions but to questions they should have gotten right under time pressure.
2. **Chapter mastery in calculus and mechanics.** Two chapters each in Maths and Physics that you can do in your sleep.
3. **Mock analysis.** Every mock you take is worth 10 mocks you did not analyse. The analysis is where the learning happens.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Buying too many books.** You will not finish them. Pick one reference book per subject and finish it.
- **Starting JEE Advanced prep before JEE Main is done.** They overlap heavily, but JEE Advanced has its own traps. Cross-pollinate later, focus on Main first.
- **Skipping NCERT Chemistry.** The questions are pulled directly from it.
- **Skipping revision.** Solving new problems feels productive. It is not. Old problems, solved correctly and repeatedly, are where marks come from.

## Final note

JEE Main is a speed-accuracy test, not an intelligence test. The students who score high are the ones who practised the most intelligently — they tracked their mistakes, revised the right things, and did not waste time on what they already knew.

A year is plenty of time. Use it well.
