If you are a coaching student in India, you deal with PDFs all day. Lecture slides. Mock test papers. Previous year questions. Handouts. The question is not whether you need PDF tools — it is which ones are worth your time.

This is a no-nonsense list. Every tool on it is genuinely free, runs in your browser, and does not try to upsell you on a "Pro plan" the moment you click download.

## What we look for in a student PDF tool

- **Free without asterisks.** No "free for first 3 files", no watermark on output, no signup wall.
- **Privacy.** Your notes are your notes. We strongly prefer tools that process files in the browser rather than uploading them to a server.
- **Mobile-friendly.** Most students open PDFs on their phones at some point.
- **Made for students**, not for office workers in 2014.

With those criteria, here is the shortlist.

## 2. Smallpdf — solid general-purpose

Smallpdf is the 800-pound gorilla of online PDF tools. It does everything: convert, compress, merge, split, edit, sign.

**Pros:** Polished interface, fast, mobile app.
**Cons:** Free tier is limited to 2 tasks per day. The "Pro" plan is ₹1,500/year. Files are uploaded to their servers.

Use it for occasional, non-sensitive tasks. Not ideal if you are processing your only copy of class notes.

## 3. iLovePDF — same trade-off as Smallpdf

iLovePDF has a similar feature set and similar limits. Free tier caps you at a few tasks per day; the paid tier is similar in price.

Their PDF editing tools (highlight, annotate, add text) are better than most. Worth knowing about if you do a lot of annotation.

## 4. Google Docs — surprisingly good for reading and light editing

Google Docs will open a PDF (as Google Drive scans it). You can then search, copy text, and edit. It is not a "PDF tool" in the strict sense, but if you want to extract text from a coaching PDF so you can paste it into Notes, this is the easiest way.

It uploads to Google Drive, so do not use it for anything sensitive.

## 5. Adobe Acrobat online — the original, but watch the limits

Adobe's online tools are the historical default. They work well, but the free tier is heavily restricted and you cannot avoid the upsell for very long. Most students will not need the full Adobe feature set anyway.

## 6. Your phone's built-in PDF tools

Modern Android and iOS phones can:

- Open and view PDFs.
- Mark up PDFs (highlight, draw, type).
- Print to PDF (save any webpage or document as PDF).
- Scan documents to PDF (using the camera).

You almost certainly have these. Use them.

## What about desktop apps?

If you are on a Windows laptop, **PDFsam Basic** is a free, open-source desktop app for merging, splitting, and reordering PDFs. It does not have the dark → light conversion feature (which is browser-only by necessity), but it is a solid backup.

On macOS, **Preview** does most of what you need natively. Open a PDF, drag pages between files to merge, etc.
