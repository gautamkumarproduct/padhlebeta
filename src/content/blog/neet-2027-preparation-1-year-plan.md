NEET 2027 is roughly a year away. That is either a lot of time or not nearly enough, depending on where you are right now.

This is a realistic plan. It assumes you are starting from a place of "I know the basics but need to consolidate." If you are starting from scratch, compress the schedule but do not skip phases.

## Phase 1: Foundation (Months 1–4)

The first four months are for **NCERT mastery**. Not "read once". Mastery.

### Biology
- Class 11 NCERT: read every line. Every diagram. Every box. The exam pulls ~70% of Biology questions directly from these books.
- Class 12 NCERT: same. Focus on genetics, biotechnology, ecology, and human physiology chapters — they are heavily tested.
- Make a "one-page summary" for every chapter. You will use these for revision later.

### Physics
- Start with **mechanics** (Class 11). It is the foundation. Concepts: Newton's laws, work-energy, rotational mechanics, gravitation.
- Move to **modern physics** and **optics** early — they have a high weight-to-difficulty ratio.
- For every numerical problem, write down: the concept, the formula, and the assumption you made. Do not just look at the solution.

### Chemistry
- Class 11: physical chemistry fundamentals (mole concept, atomic structure, bonding, thermodynamics).
- Class 12: organic chemistry basics (GOC, hydrocarbons, functional groups) and physical chemistry (electrochemistry, kinetics, solutions).
- Inorganic: NCERT is gospel. Read it twice.

### Daily rhythm (Phase 1)
- 6–8 hours of focused study. Less than that and you are not preparing seriously; more than that and you will burn out by month 4.
- 70% new learning, 30% revision of what you covered in the previous week.
- One subject per day as the main focus, with short touch-ups on the other two.

## Phase 2: Application (Months 5–8)

NCERT is no longer the bottleneck. Now you need to **apply** what you learned.

- **Mock tests every Sunday.** Full-length, timed, simulated exam conditions. No phone. No breaks beyond the official ones.
- **Topic-wise problem sets** every weekday. Pick a chapter, do 30 problems from it. Mix difficulty levels.
- **Doubt-clearing schedule:** one fixed slot per week for solving doubts with a teacher or study partner. Do not let doubts pile up.

### Subject focus in Phase 2

**Biology:** Move from NCERT-only to reference books (Trueman's, MTG). Diagram-based questions and assertion-reason type questions appear here.

**Physics:** Problem-solving speed is the differentiator. Practice 20–30 numericals a day. Time yourself.

**Chemistry:** Organic becomes more important. Mechanisms, named reactions, and reagent-level understanding.

### Daily rhythm (Phase 2)
- 8–10 hours.
- Two subjects in depth per day, one for revision.
- Sunday: full mock + analysis (the analysis is more important than the mock itself).

## Phase 3: Consolidation (Months 9–11)

You should be doing well in mock tests by now. The goal of Phase 3 is to **convert "I know this" into "I cannot get this wrong"**.

- **Error log.** Every wrong answer in every mock goes into a spreadsheet with: topic, why you got it wrong (silly mistake / concept gap / time pressure), and what you will do differently.
- **Revision cycles.** Every 30 days, revise all your one-page summaries from scratch. Anything that does not come back instantly goes back on the priority list.
- **Subject-specific deep dives.** Identify your weakest subject and give it 50% more time than the others.

### Daily rhythm (Phase 3)
- 9–11 hours.
- 50% revision, 30% new problems, 20% mock tests.

## Phase 4: Peak performance (Final 4–6 weeks)

The syllabus is done. The goal now is to **not lose marks**.

- **No new chapters.** Resist the urge to "just cover" something. You will lose more marks by panicking than by skipping.
- **Daily mock + analysis.** Alternate between full mocks and subject-specific mocks.
- **Sleep is non-negotiable.** 7 hours minimum. Your brain consolidates memory during sleep. Cutting sleep in the final month is the single biggest mistake students make.
- **Light revision only.** One-page summaries. NCERT diagrams. Formula sheets.

### Last week
- Stop intense studying 2 days before the exam.
- Light revision, early sleep, hydration, calm.

## Resources that actually help

- **NCERT.** Non-negotiable. Read it, mark it, reread it.
- **One reference book per subject.** Do not buy five books for each subject. You will read none of them.
- **Previous year papers (last 10 years).** Solve them. Notice the patterns.
- **One mock test series.** Any of the big names (Allen, Aakash, PW test series) work. Pick one and stick with it.

## What to skip

- Coaching motivational lectures.
- "Topper strategies" YouTube videos.
- Every PDF you can get your hands on. Quality > quantity.
- Comparing your preparation with others.

## A note on mental health

This is a one-year grind. You will have bad weeks. You will have bad mocks. That is normal. What matters is not the bad day — it is whether you get back to the desk the next morning.

If you are consistently anxious, sleeping poorly, or losing interest in things you used to enjoy, talk to someone. A counsellor, a parent, a friend, a teacher. NEET is important. It is not more important than you.

## The bottom line

A year is plenty of time for NEET preparation if you use it well. NCERT first, mocks early, revision relentlessly, sleep properly. You will be fine.
