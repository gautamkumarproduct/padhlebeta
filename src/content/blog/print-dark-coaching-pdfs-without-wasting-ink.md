If you have ever tried to print a 200-page batch of coaching notes and watched your ink cartridge empty halfway through, you already know the problem. Most lecture slides are designed for screens — black background, white text. They look slick on a projector, but they are a disaster on paper.

Here is the complete guide to printing them without going broke.

## Why dark slides waste so much ink

Inkjet printers deposit colour onto paper. When a page is mostly **dark background with light text**, the printer is essentially covering the page in ink. A single dark slide can use 5–10× the ink of the equivalent light slide.

Coaching PDFs are not malicious — they are just built for screens. The good news: you do not need to give them up. You just need to invert them before printing.

## The fastest fix: convert dark to light first

The single biggest ink saving comes from inverting the colours of every page in the PDF. White background, dark text. Same content, fraction of the ink.

## Set your printer to "draft" or "economode"

Even on a converted (light-background) PDF, printer settings matter:

- **Draft / EconoMode:** Uses less ink per page. Slightly lower quality but very readable. Worth it for review prints.
- **Black & white / Greyscale:** Most coaching slides are essentially black text on a coloured background. If you have already inverted to white background, the slides may look fine in pure greyscale.
- **Single-sided:** Unless you really need double-sided notes, print single-sided. Double-sided forces the printer to lay down ink for both pages in close proximity, which can muddy text.
- **Skip blank pages:** Most coaching slides have empty filler pages. Many printers have a "skip blank pages" option in the driver settings.

## Pick the right paper

Standard 80 GSM A4 is fine for most uses. If you are writing notes on top of printed slides, **75 GSM is actually better** — your pen shows through slightly, but it costs less per page.

If you have access to a laser printer (school, library, cyber cafe), use it for big batches. Laser toner is far cheaper per page than inkjet.

## Print in batches, not everything at once

A common mistake: students try to print a whole chapter (300+ pages) in one go. Two problems:

1. The printer overheats and slows down.
2. You find out at page 250 that the chapter has a typo and you need to fix something.

Print 30–50 pages at a time. Review. Continue. It sounds slow, but it saves you from printing pages you do not need.

## What about the diagrams and charts?

This is the catch with converting dark slides to light: any embedded **logos, charts, or photographs** will also be inverted. White-on-blue becomes blue-on-white. The result is usually still readable, but it can look odd.

If a particular slide has critical diagrams (say, a circuit diagram or a chemistry mechanism), consider:

- Re-finding the original slide on the coaching platform (they often have downloadable PDFs separately).
- Skipping that one slide and printing the rest.
- Using the un-converted PDF for that one chapter only.

In practice, this affects maybe 5% of slides for most coaching material.

## The summary

1. Convert dark slides to light first. This is the single biggest saving.
2. Set your printer to draft / econo / greyscale.
3. Print in batches.
4. Use the right paper (or laser printer when available).
5. Be selective about slides with diagrams.
